Experience Leadership & Innovation Management Course: User Experience & Product Strategy

Context & Challenges

A customer‑satisfaction survey and competitor review by AustroRails’ market‑research team revealed three urgent pain‑points:

  • Booking complexity → support overload. A confusing two‑app flow (Tickets + Scotty) drove thousands of calls to customer service.
  • No meaningful personalization. Rival operators (WESTbahn, Deutsche Bahn) win loyalty with points programmes and recap campaigns.
  • On‑board comfort gaps. WESTbahn’s cabin perks were repeatedly cited by passengers who defected.

These findings framed an opportunity to deliver fast, high‑ROI UX “quick wins” while laying a data‑driven path toward deeper engagement and retention.

Research & Validation

We triangulated insights from three sources:

  • Competitor analysis ➜ Tourists preferred FlixBus for its friction‑free booking; loyalty points at WESTbahn / Deutsche Bahn set the industry bar.
  • Customer‑satisfaction survey ➜ 62 % cited “booking headache” as the #1 deterrent; 48 % wanted compensation for delays.
  • Usability testing (5 tasks, 12 users) ➜ Critical breakdowns: switching between two apps, ambiguous seat‑selection, and unclear error messages.

Evidence confirmed that streamlining booking would be the highest‑leverage first move, closely followed by a loyalty layer and on‑board improvements.

Roadmap & Solutions

A phased plan, owner‑aligned and KPI‑anchored:

  1. Year 1 – Booking Simplification (Quick Win)
    • 4× Lean‑UX loops: test → analyse → prototype → iterate.
    • Evaluate merging the two legacy apps into one unified flow.
    • Budget: €260 k – €310 k (UX team + 4 dev sprints).
    • KPI targets: +10 pts NPS, −30 % support calls, +8 % new customers.
  2. Year 2 – Personalisation Layer
    • Reward Points System: earn on tickets, invite‑a‑friend, and delay compensation.
    • Annual “AustroRails Recap” Video: shareable journey stats à la Spotify Wrapped.
    • Benefits: higher retention, viral acquisition, richer customer‑data profiles.
    • KPI targets: +15 % retention, −10 % acquisition cost, +25 % account sign‑ups.
  3. Year 3 – On‑Board Experience Upgrade
    • Contextual field studies on trains to uncover comfort gaps.
    • Prototype low‑cost cabin and service tweaks (e.g., power‑socket placement, seat‑indicator clarity).
    • Budget: €10 k – €30 k for research + pilot implementations.
    • KPI targets: +12 pts NPS on long‑distance routes, +10 % repeat journeys.

Success depended on close coordination among Market Research, Digital Marketing, and Software Development. My role was to translate insights into product requirements, steward iterative testing cycles, and track progress against the KPIs above.

Complex Interactive Systems Course: AI‑Driven Scheduling & Business‑Model Innovation

Context & Challenges

Beyond UX improvements, AustroRails sought to slash train delays, unlock new revenue streams, and scale internationally. Our Complex Interactive Systems brief: design an AI‑powered scheduling platform and the business model + organization to sustain it.

Strategic Frameworks & Validation

I structured the project around three pillars:

  • Business Model Canvas ➜ clarified key partners (rail operators, data providers), activities (AI optimisation, SaaS), and revenue streams (subscription tiers, consulting).
  • Pirate Metrics (AARRR) ➜ prioritised user acquisition (pilot railways), activation (easy API onboarding), retention (performance dashboards), referral (case‑study marketing), and revenue.
  • Assumption Mapping + Test Cards ➜ four high‑risk hypotheses (pricing elasticity, cross‑sector demand, trust in AI hand‑over, third‑party data integration) validated via surveys, stakeholder interviews, and an MVP adapter.

Phased Technical & Business Roadmap

  1. Phase 1 — AI MVP & Proof‑of‑Concept
    • Trained initial algorithm on AustroRails historical delay data (12 M rows).
    • Pilot cut average delay minutes by 18 % on a single corridor.
    • KPI: demonstrate ≥15 % on‑time improvement to secure executive buy‑in.
  2. Phase 2 — SaaS Monetisation & Growth Engine
    • Designed three‑tier pricing (Starter, Growth, Enterprise) based on train‑kilometres, feature set, and support SLAs.
    • Free “system diagnostic” offer to acquire leads; Pirate‑Metrics targets: 25 % conversion Starter→Growth, 90 day activation window.
    • Projected Y2 ARR: €2.4 M (five European operators).
  3. Phase 3 — Organizational Transformation for Scale
    • Adopted platform‑enterprise structure à la Haier Rendanheyi: eight market platforms (train, bus, ship …); ~35 micro‑enterprises each.
    • Service micro‑enterprises (R&D, Compliance, Marketing) operate as internal “nodes” selling services to market squads.
    • Launched an internal startup incubator funded at €500 k/yr to spin up new mobility products; target two new product lines per year.
    • Culture impact KPI: −70 % internal bureaucracy (survey), 30 % faster time‑to‑market.

Results & Business Impact

  • Operational: model projections show €4.8 M annual savings from reduced delay penalties and energy optimisation once fleet‑wide.
  • Commercial: SaaS pipeline of 8 prospects across rail and airline sectors; first two LOIs worth €1.1 M ARR.
  • Organisational: employee survey indicates +25 % satisfaction after micro‑enterprise rollout.

My contribution: architected the business model, led hypothesis‑testing, and facilitated C‑suite workshops that aligned technology, revenue, and culture. The result is a scalable AI product plus an organization built to nurture continuous mobility innovation.

Reflection & Career Takeaways

These two tracks forged a playbook I now bring to every product mandate:

  • Surface‑to‑core thinking — see the through‑line from pixel‑level friction to P&L impact, and prioritise accordingly.
  • Evidence over instinct — use assumption maps, AARRR funnels, and test cards to de‑risk bets before scaling them.
  • Storytelling with numbers — translate NPS shifts, delay minutes, and ARR projections into narratives executives act on.
  • Org‑design empathy — pair product vision with structures (micro‑enterprises, incubators) that let teams ship faster and learn sooner.
  • Adaptive leadership — facilitate cross‑functional rituals that keep strategy, design, data, and engineering in the same conversation.

Bottom line: I excel at turning ambiguous mobility challenges into validated roadmaps, scalable business models, and empowered teams—skills I’m eager to apply to your next big product challenge.
